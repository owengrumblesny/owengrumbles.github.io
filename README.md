## Article IV — Public Development and Historical Transparency

### 4.1. Public Availability

The author intends the repository and its development history to remain publicly accessible, subject to the availability and operation of the hosting platform.

The repository may contain source code, articles, documentation, and other materials whose revisions are recorded through version control.

Visitors may inspect available commits, compare revisions, and examine changes to tracked files in order to understand how the project has developed.

### 4.2. Historical Accountability

The author considers an accessible development history to be useful for evaluating the evolution of the project and the decisions made in its maintenance.

Where changes to website code, articles, or licensing notices are recorded in the public repository, such records may assist readers in determining what changed and when.

This arrangement is intended to facilitate scrutiny rather than to establish a claim of infallibility. Published materials may be revised, arguments may be reconsidered, and errors may be corrected. The existence of an earlier version is not, by itself, proof that the earlier version was superior.

Readers are invited to examine the available record and draw their own conclusions.

### 4.3. Limitations of Version Control

The author acknowledges that Git history is not an immutable public archive. Repository history may be rewritten, files may be removed, and access or visibility may change. Copies and forks may preserve material independently, but their continued availability cannot be guaranteed.

Accordingly, this article expresses an intention toward public transparency; it does not warrant that every historical version will remain permanently accessible or that every possible alteration will be detectable.

The distinction between an aspiration to transparency and a technically guaranteed impossibility of concealment is acknowledged expressly.
